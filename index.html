Evidence Research

When averages mislead: diagnostic gaps in education programme management

Live data from 312 students · 14 Delhi NCR centres · JEE Main 2026 Session 1 · Prepared for J-PAL South Asia

Finding 1

The Mean–Median Trap

A 9.9-point gap exposes a struggling lower tail invisible to headline reporting

Implication: 11.8% of students fell below the 50th percentile — invisible to programme managers tracking only mean performance. By the time the tail appears in summary stats, the intervention window has closed.

Finding 2

Resource Allocation Paradox

The strongest subject is also the highest-leverage one — not the weakest

Implication: Physics (r = 0.90, 81% of outcome variance) outperforms Maths (r = 0.66, 44%) as a predictor despite having the highest average score. Deficit-only remediation misallocates resources.

Finding 3

Centre-Level Variance

Same curriculum, same training — 4× difference in JEE Advanced conversion

Implication: The performance gap appears institutionally produced, not student-determined. Top-centre practices are identifiable, codifiable, and replicable — a high-return quality intervention for any education network.

Finding 4

Recognition System Failure

35 students crossed the 99th percentile without formal recognition — 2.5× the 14 who were tagged

Implication: When recognition lags performance, the institution's model of "who is succeeding" becomes systematically inaccurate — corrupting scholarship allocation, resource planning, and external programme claims.

Evidence Note

When Averages Mislead: Diagnostic Gaps in India's Competitive Exam Preparation Ecosystem

JEE Main 2026 · Programme-Level Analysis · Delhi NCR
Submitted: J-PAL South Asia Policy Manager Application · June 2025

The central argument: most education programmes track outcomes but not the leading indicators that enable early, effective intervention. By the time a student's total performance looks weak, the root cause is often 12–18 months upstream — a gap in foundational understanding that compounded quietly while the programme watched a stable average. The institutions that improve fastest are not those with the strongest students; they are those with the strongest diagnostic systems. This evidence note proposes four tractable, data-addressable levers: distributional reporting, correlation-informed resource allocation, centre-level benchmarking for practice sharing, and dynamic recognition systems updated on live performance data.
